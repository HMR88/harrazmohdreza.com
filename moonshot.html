Sector A · The monkey, not the pedestal

What could kill this hire?

The riskiest assumption goes first. That is the whole method. Open each one and decide for yourself whether it is still a risk.

0/5 Risks de-risked

De-risk:Tenure measures time. The role measures whether I can de-risk technology and business at once, and depth beats a decade of the same year repeated.

  • Technician to Technical Program Manager in six years, promoted at nearly every stop, not by waiting my turn but by taking scope no one asked me to.
  • "Deep tech" at X is disproportionately spatial: grids, connectivity, climate. Location isn't my niche. It's the substrate those moonshots sit on.
  • The gap is the pitch. If X only wants people who already fit the pattern, it isn't casting a net for moonshots. It's hiring a resume.

De-risk:The monkey-first instinct is already on the record.

  • Found the defect generating 53 million phantom candidate locations inside a network tool and cut its runtime from over an hour to minutes. That is a 60x leap, not a 10% tune-up.
  • Ran QA and acceptance on 500,000+ wildfire-prevention maps across 14 departments, where a silent wrong answer is a public-safety event, not a bug ticket.
  • Built the Python and SQL pipelines (Azure to Esri) and the governance behind them, designing against the quiet failures before they happen.

De-risk:This is the rare half. Most technical people can't do it. Most business people can't find the defect above. I have both on the same résumé.

  • Grew one account 340%, from $201K to $888K, by scoping the opportunity myself, and locked a 12-month renewal.
  • Owned a $2.9M portfolio across three concurrent engagements: scope, staffing, budget, delivery, QBRs, change-orders.
  • "It'd be great if you could de-risk from both the technical and business perspectives." That line in your posting is a description of my last two years.

De-risk:I carry things through adoption. Ideas are cheap. Enacted change is rare.

  • Architected and launched a flagship enterprise application and web portal from inception, giving teams self-service maps instead of filing tickets and waiting.
  • Ship live tools people operate themselves: an event-driven router that killed a daily manual triage step with 100% record coverage, and a Power BI system that scores 3,600+ records a day and emails each manager their slice.
  • The thing you're reading is the proof. Most candidates send a PDF. I built you an instrument.

De-risk:Give me a mess and a decision that has to be made anyway.

  • Turned coverage data from 90+ inconsistent providers into an analysis that informed $51M in state and federal broadband funding. Chaos in, public infrastructure out.
  • My working principles were earned on real data: build for the messy reality, verify against primary sources, prevent the silent wrong answers first.
  • I fall in love with problems, not solutions, which is why I'd rather be told I'm early than told I'm right.
Assessment complete

Every risk that would kill this hire is now on the table, and answered in your own hands. The remaining question isn't whether I fit the posting. It's whether X is brave enough to talk to the candidate who doesn't.
